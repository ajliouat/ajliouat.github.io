What you trade

Part one ended on the cost of the open-weights way: tens of gigabytes of weights per stage, on the defaults of its calculator 127 GPU hours on an L4 for one finished minute, a licence matrix to review, and a person watching every generation. The managed services sell the same outputs per second, per character or per seat. The GPUs and much of the quality work become theirs. What stays yours is the brief, the review, and the terms of what you generate. What you give up is control: over the model, its version, and whether it still exists next month.

A month of churn

That last point is not hypothetical. On 24 September 2026, OpenAI shut down the Sora 2 models and its Videos API, with no one-to-one replacement. On 22 October 2026, the Veo 3.1 preview models were shut down on the Gemini API, where Google now points to Gemini Omni Flash; the generally available Veo 3.1 models remain on Google Cloud’s agent platform. Runway retired Gen-4 Aleph on 30 July 2026. A pipeline built on a service has to be built to change service.

Rights and training

The price is the easy part to compare. The terms differ more: who owns the output, whether your scripts and footage train the vendor’s next model, and whether the free tier can be used for work at all. Of the services above, Synthesia states it does not train on customer content, ElevenLabs offers an opt-out, Google’s paid Gemini API tier does not use prompts or responses to improve its products, and HeyGen and Suno grant themselves training rights with no opt-out described. For a company, those lines matter more than a cent per second.

Choosing a way

What this part does not establish

  • Prices and terms are those on the vendors’ pages on 25 October 2026. Some pages could not be read in full; the table says so where it matters.
  • The calculator is arithmetic on public prices and your assumptions, not a quote.
  • No service was tested for this article, and the quality of their outputs is not compared.
  • Some prices could only be read in search results, such as Google Cloud’s Veo pages and Kling’s own API page; they are left out, or given through a reseller’s page.
